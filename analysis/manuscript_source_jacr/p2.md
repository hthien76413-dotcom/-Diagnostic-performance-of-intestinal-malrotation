#H1 Results

#H2 Cohort and children without an index test

#N Of 450 children with surgically confirmed malrotation, 300 (66.7%) were neonates and 397 (88.2%) had midgut volvulus (Table 1). An index examination was available in 293 children for the UGI series, 313 for CT and 117 for ultrasound; 398 underwent at least one and 59 all three. The remaining 52 (11.6%) underwent none of the three, but all had other preoperative imaging; in four it was documented before transfer (Table 1, Figure 1).

#N The modality groups were not interchangeable (Table 1). Ultrasound was performed more often in children with volvulus, its use rising from 13% to 51% of operated children between eras. Among children who underwent more than one index test, the UGI series was the last preoperative test in 75.0% of those who received it against 19.2% for CT, and UGI detection was higher when it came last (80.0% [144/180] vs 70.0% [42/60] when it did not), a feature of the pathway rather than of the test.

#H2 Detection and certainty of wording

#N Detection was 230/293 (78.5%, 95% CI 73.4–82.8) for the UGI series, 165/313 (52.7%, 47.2–58.2) for CT and 64/117 (54.7%, 45.7–63.4) for ultrasound (Table 2, Figure 2). In a GEE model adjusted for era and age group, CT and ultrasound remained less often positive than the UGI series; the coefficients absorb indication, pathway position and examination content and are given in Supplement 2. Reclassifying possible-tier conclusions as negative reduced detection to 50.2%, 32.9% and 25.6%; counting only conclusions that named malrotation gave 77.8%, 48.6% and 53.8% (Table 2).

#H2 Documented content of ultrasound examinations

#N Of the 117 ultrasound index examinations, three (2.6%; 95% CI 0.9–7.3) documented D3 or the duodenojejunal junction and seven (6.0%) mentioned the duodenum (Table 3, Figure 3). Three (2.6%) stated the mesenteric artery–vein relationship, and only one stated that the vessels were inverted, one of the two ultrasound positivity criteria. Enteric fluid was recorded twice and graded compression once; a whirlpool was reported in 59 examinations (50.4%) and bowel gas limited 36 (30.8%). Reader agreement was 1,423 of 1,428 item codes in the 119 episodes read (kappa 0.80–1.00; Supplement 1).

#N Of these three, only one, in 2022, followed D3 between the artery and the aorta and identified the duodenojejunal junction, as the published technique describes [16, 17].

#N The diagnosis was named in all 59 examinations recording a whirlpool and in 5 of the 58 without one (8.6%, 95% CI 3.7–18.6). This contrast is partly definitional, as a whirlpool is itself a positivity criterion, although all 59 conclusions also named malrotation explicitly. A whirlpool was recorded in only 59 of the 112 children (52.7%) in whom operation confirmed midgut volvulus.

#H2 Change in detection over time

#N In this exploratory analysis, ultrasound detection was 42.1% (16/38) in 2012–2018 and 60.8% (48/79) in 2019–2026, a difference of +18.7 percentage points whose 95% CI (−0.2 to +37.7) includes zero; CT detection rose and UGI detection did not (Table 4). Sessions including a great-vessel study rose from 34.2% to 68.4%, and adding this booking indicator reduced the ultrasound difference to +8.4 (−10.6 to +29.2) percentage points (Supplement 2). Whirlpool reporting rose from 39.5% to 55.7%, whereas D3 or the duodenojejunal junction was documented in one and two examinations.

#H2 Selected subgroup and populations not represented

#N Fifty-nine children underwent all three modalities. This subgroup, presumably selected by diagnostic uncertainty, was more severely affected than the other 339 imaged children (volvulus 96.6% vs 87.9%; neonates 83.1% vs 67.3%). The UGI series was positive in 76.3%, ultrasound in 52.5% and CT in 45.8% (Cochran Q test, *P* < .001; UGI series minus CT +30.5 percentage points, 95% CI +13.6 to +47.5; CT minus ultrasound −6.8, −20.3 to +8.5), a pattern unchanged when all three examinations fell within 48 h (Supplement 2). Only 5 of the 53 children with malrotation but no volvulus underwent ultrasound, and none had a positive report; a Firth penalized model supports the direction of this contrast but not its magnitude (Supplement 2).
