#H1 Results

#H2 Cohort and children without an index test

#N Of 450 children with surgically confirmed malrotation, 300 (66.7%) were neonates and 397 (88.2%) had midgut volvulus (Table 1). An index examination was available in 293 children for the UGI series, 313 for CT and 117 for ultrasound; 398 underwent at least one and 59 all three. The remaining 52 (11.6%) underwent none of the three, but all had other preoperative imaging; in four it was documented before transfer (Table 1, Figure 1).

#N The modality groups were not interchangeable (Table 1). Ultrasound was performed more often in children with volvulus, its use rising from 13% to 51% of operated children between eras. Among children who underwent more than one index test, the UGI series was the last preoperative test in 75.0% of those who received it against 19.2% for CT, and detection was higher in that position (80.0% vs 70.0% for the UGI series), a feature of the pathway rather than of the test.

#H2 Detection and certainty of wording

#N Detection was 230/293 (78.5%, 95% CI 73.4–82.8) for the UGI series, 165/313 (52.7%, 47.2–58.2) for CT and 64/117 (54.7%, 45.7–63.4) for ultrasound (Table 2, Figure 2). These indication-selected, overlapping denominators are not a ranking. CT and ultrasound had lower odds of a positive report than the UGI series (adjusted odds ratio 0.29 and 0.27), and the same model showed higher detection above 1 year of age (3.52, 1.82–6.80) and in the later era (1.79, 1.18–2.71); these coefficients absorb indication, pathway position and examination content (Supplement 2). Reclassifying possible-tier conclusions as negative reduced detection to 50.5%, 32.9% and 27.4% (Table 2).

#H2 Documented content of ultrasound examinations

#N Of the 117 ultrasound index examinations, three (2.6%; 95% CI 0.9–7.3) documented D3 or the duodenojejunal junction and seven (6.0%) mentioned the duodenum (Table 3, Figure 3). Three (2.6%) stated the mesenteric artery–vein relationship, and one stated that the vessels were inverted, although inversion was one of the two positivity criteria. Enteric fluid was recorded twice and graded compression once; a whirlpool was reported in 59 examinations (50.4%) and bowel gas limited 36 (30.8%). Reader agreement was 1,423 of 1,428 item codes (kappa 0.80–1.00; Supplement 1).

#N In 2018 the horizontal duodenum was described passing anterior to the superior mesenteric artery after oral contrast; in 2022 it was followed between the artery and the aorta with the duodenojejunal junction identified, the only examination resembling the published protocol; in 2024 it could not be filled and further imaging was advised.

#N Detection followed this content closely. Malrotation was reported in 59 of the 59 examinations recording a whirlpool and in 5 of the 58 without one (8.6%, 95% CI 3.7–18.6). This contrast is partly definitional, as a whirlpool is itself a positivity criterion, and all 59 of those conclusions named malrotation or volvulus explicitly. The converse is more informative: a whirlpool was recorded in only 59 of the 112 children (52.7%) in whom operation confirmed midgut volvulus.

#H2 Change in detection over time

#N Ultrasound detection was 42.1% (16/38) in 2012–2018 and 60.8% (48/79) in 2019–2026, a difference of +18.7 percentage points (95% CI −0.2 to +37.7) that is itself imprecise; detection rose for CT but not the UGI series (Table 4). For ultrasound the change tracked how the examination was booked. Sessions including a great-vessel study rose from 34.2% to 68.4%, and adding this indicator reduced the era effect to +8.4 (−10.6 to +29.2) percentage points, the indicator carrying an odds ratio of 3.51 (1.56–7.87). Moving the era boundary from 2019 to 2022 left the examination-type odds ratio between 3.21 and 4.04, while the era odds ratio ranged from 2.66 to 1.17 (Supplement 2). The era-by-booking interaction was not significant, in small subgroups. The change in booking tracked whirlpool reporting but not duodenal assessment. CT showed a comparable change that contrast enhancement explained only in part (Table 4).

#H2 Selected subgroup and populations not represented

#N Fifty-nine children underwent all three modalities. This subgroup, in whom the diagnosis remained uncertain, was more severely affected than the other 339 imaged children (volvulus 96.6% vs 87.9%; neonates 83.1% vs 67.3%). The UGI series was positive in 76.3%, ultrasound in 52.5% and CT in 45.8% (Cochran's Q p<0.001; UGI series minus CT +30.5 percentage points, 95% CI +13.6 to +47.5; CT minus ultrasound −6.8, −20.3 to +8.5), unchanged within 48 h (Supplement 2). These comparisons describe reporting within one selected subgroup and are not measures of accuracy. Only 5 of the 53 children with malrotation but no volvulus underwent ultrasound, and none had a positive report; a Firth penalized model supports the direction of this contrast but not its magnitude (Supplement 2).
