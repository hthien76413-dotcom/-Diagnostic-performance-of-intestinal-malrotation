#N **Short / running title**：Duodenal Landmarks in Routine Malrotation Ultrasound Reports

#N **Abstract**：从正文复制四段（含段名），共 249 词。**Keywords 不要粘进摘要框。**
#N Objectives: Ultrasound is increasingly advocated as the first-line test for suspected intestinal malrotation, based on reported sensitivities of 93–97%. We audited what routine reports documented and whether they named the diagnosis.
#N Methods: Retrospective case-only report audit at one tertiary children's hospital (December 2012–June 2026). The preoperative examination closest to operation was classified by a rule-based algorithm with blinded surgical adjudication, and audited for documented technical content (ultrasound by two independent readers). As the cohort was surgically confirmed, the outcome was report-level detection among tested children, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals; specificity, predictive values and comparative accuracy are not estimable.
#N Results: Among 450 children (volvulus in 88.2%), 398 underwent at least one index test. Three of 117 ultrasound examinations (2.6%, 95% confidence interval 0.9–7.3) documented the third portion of the duodenum or the duodenojejunal junction, 3 (2.6%) the mesenteric artery–vein relationship and 2 (1.7%) enteric fluid; a whirlpool sign was reported in 50.4%. Ultrasound named malrotation or volvulus in 64 of 117 examinations (54.7%, 45.7–63.4): in all 59 recording a whirlpool, a positivity criterion, and in 5 of 58 without one. A whirlpool was recorded in 59 of the 112 ultrasound-examined children with operatively confirmed volvulus. Detection was 53.8% counting only conclusions naming malrotation, 25.6% excluding "possible" wording.
#N Conclusions: Positive routine ultrasound reports almost always described a whirlpool, and the duodenal landmarks to which published performance is attributed were rarely documented. Documentation, unlike accuracy, can be audited without a reference standard; we provide the criteria as a minimum reporting dataset.

#N **Keywords**：Intestinal malrotation; Intestinal volvulus; Infant, Newborn; Ultrasonography; Radiology report

#N • At one center, routine ultrasound reports for children with surgically confirmed malrotation (96% with volvulus) documented the duodenal landmarks emphasized by published series in 2.6% of 117 examinations, and positive reports almost always described a whirlpool (59 of 64).
