#H1 S2.2 Detection of a volvulus-specific sign among children with surgically confirmed midgut volvulus

#TABS2B

#N A volvulus-specific sign was defined as a whirlpool or corkscrew appearance, or an explicit report of a twist. In the subgroup of 57 children with confirmed volvulus who received all three examinations, ultrasound and the UGI series reported such a sign at similar rates and both numerically more often than CT, but no pairwise comparison was robust: under the pre-specified text definition used here, ultrasound versus CT gave p=0.13 and Cochran's Q p=0.12. An earlier version of this analysis, using a narrower sign definition, produced a nominally significant ultrasound-versus-CT difference (p=0.041). Because the result is sensitive to how the sign is defined, we report it here as exploratory and make no claim of a difference between modalities.

#N The finding that survives this sensitivity is directional rather than comparative: within ultrasound, the whirlpool sign was both the dominant documented finding and the near-exclusive determinant of a positive report (main manuscript, Table 3), which is consistent with the whirlpool sign being fundamentally a sign of volvulus rather than of malrotation.

#T Online Resource 3. Content audit of the CT and upper gastrointestinal series index reports

#N Supplement to the same manuscript.

#TABS3

#N Content was coded from the findings and conclusion text of each index report using the patterns in Online Resource 1, Section H. Detection percentages are report-level detection among surgically confirmed children, conditional on whether the element was documented. As with the ultrasound audit in the main manuscript, these are associations within routine reports and are not adjusted for indication, age or era; a report describing a mesenteric whirl is, unsurprisingly, a report likely to conclude that malrotation is present, and the value of the tabulation is in showing how often each element was documented at all.

#N Two observations are worth noting alongside the ultrasound findings. First, the upper gastrointestinal series was the only modality whose routine reports documented the anatomical landmark that defines malrotation — the duodenojejunal junction — in a majority of examinations (54.5%), and jejunal position in 70.4%. Second, the mesenteric whirl was documented in only 36.2% of CT reports, and contrast enhancement, which is what renders the whirl visible, was used in only 16.2%; the two together account for most of the observed CT detection rate.
